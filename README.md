### 🦋 PROJECT 01 — THE RIVERS PROJECT

### ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**England's rivers have been keeping a secret for 30 years. I read it.**

Macroinvertebrates — the tiny creatures living in riverbeds — are one of the most sensitive ecological indicators on earth. When they disappear, something is wrong. When they thrive, something is right.

The Environment Agency has been recording them for over three decades. Nobody had looked at the full national picture.

Until this project.

The data was a nightmare — mixed formats, inconsistent sampling, thousands of sites, missing values everywhere. I built reproducible pipelines to harmonise it all, then applied **Generalised Additive Models** to let the long-term patterns emerge without forcing a shape onto them.

What came out was a national-scale story about water quality, ecological change, and what three decades of monitoring can actually tell us — if you know how to ask.

```
📦 Skills inside this repo you won't find in tutorials:
   ├── Wrangling genuinely messy, multi-decade ecological data
   ├── GAMs — when linear models aren't enough
   ├── Building pipelines other researchers can actually reuse
   └── Making statistical outputs readable to non-statisticians
```

`Python` · `GAMs` · `Pandas` · `Matplotlib` · `Statistical Modelling` · `Environmental Science`

**10,000+ seismic observations. One question: what actually drives the risk?**

This wasn't a Kaggle competition. This was an industry collaboration — real regulators, real stakes, real data that arrived messy and multi-sourced and needed to *mean something* to people who don't speak statistics.

The challenge wasn't building a model that performs well. It was building one that **explains itself** — that tells a regulator *which* operational variables matter and *why*, in language they can act on.

The answer came through **Generalised Additive Models** — flexible enough to capture non-linear patterns, interpretable enough to trust.

```
📦 Skills inside this repo you won't find in tutorials:
   ├── Integrating heterogeneous multi-source time-series data
   ├── Explainability-first modelling for non-technical audiences
   ├── Feature engineering under real-world constraints
   └── Communicating uncertainty honestly
```

`Python` · `GAMs` · `Time Series` · `Feature Engineering` · `Risk Analytics` · `Industry Collaboration`

**Every hour someone waits in A&E, there's a data trail. I followed it.**

NHS A&E performance data is public. Everyone talks about the "4-hour target". Fewer people have sat down with years of attendance records and asked: *where* is the system breaking, *when* does it happen, and *what* does the pattern look like?

This project does exactly that — building a complete analytical pipeline from raw public data to findings that are accessible to anyone who wants to understand the pressure England's emergency care system is under.

### ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**Most A/B tests are run wrong. This one isn't.**

P-hacking. Peeking at results early. Underpowered tests that can't detect what they're looking for. These are quiet, widespread problems in data-driven decision making — and most tutorials don't teach you to avoid them.

This project is a rigorous end-to-end experimentation framework — power analysis, experimental design, hypothesis testing, multiple comparison correction — built not just to produce a result, but to produce one you can *trust*.

```
📦 Skills inside this repo you won't find in tutorials:
   ├── Proper power analysis before you collect a single data point
   ├── Multiple testing corrections (Bonferroni, BH)
   ├── Knowing when NOT to reject the null hypothesis
   └── Communicating statistical uncertainty without losing people
```

`Python` · `SciPy` · `Statsmodels` · `Pandas` · `Statistical Inference` · `Experimental Design`
